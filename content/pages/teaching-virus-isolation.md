The Virus Isolation Course is a phage isolation course and the first semester of the two-semester [Phage Discovery Program](/teaching/phage-discovery), Tarleton State University's part of SEA-PHAGES. It is taught as Special Topics (BIOL 4090). Undergraduates find a new bacteriophage in an environmental sample, purify it, image it and extract its DNA. The second semester is the [Phage Bioinformatics course](/teaching/phage-bioinformatics).

## What students do

1. Collect soil or water samples and bring them back to the lab.
2. Run direct and enriched isolation assays to find bacteriophages.
3. Purify each phage by serial dilutions followed by plaque assays.
4. Amplify the purified phage to a high titer.
5. Reverse stain the phage on a mesh grid and image it by transmission electron microscopy, then analyze the images to determine its morphology and type.
6. Extract the viral DNA and run a genetic analysis.

The bench methods are in the [phage isolation protocol](/research/protocols/phage-isolation) and the [phage DNA extraction protocol](/research/protocols/phage-dna-extraction). The dilutions, titers and other lab math are worked through in [Lab Calculations and Common Questions](/teaching/virus-isolation/faq).

## What students finish with

By the end of the semester each student has an isolated, purified phage amplified to a high titer, electron micrographs showing its morphology, and extracted phage DNA. If the DNA is unique and of high quality and quantity, it is sent to the Pittsburgh Bacteriophage Institute for next-generation whole genome sequencing, and that genome is what students annotate in the [Phage Bioinformatics course](/teaching/phage-bioinformatics). The phages found so far are listed in the [phage table](/research/phages).

## Safety training

The course begins with the lab safety section of the SEA-PHAGES Phage Discovery Guide, which sets universal guidelines: appropriate dress, personal protective equipment use, professional behavior, proper organization, and correct disposal of lab materials.

Before the first class session, students complete:

- [CITI Program](https://about.citiprogram.org/) 116651: Basic Introduction to Biosafety
- Texas A&M University System [TrainTraq](https://traintraq.tamus.edu/) 2111743: Lab Safety
- TrainTraq 2112788: Biosafety Level 1 Training

An additional on-site safety training covers lab-specific hazards, the location of safety equipment and evacuation routes, and students sign an acknowledgement that they have completed all safety training by the first class session. Lab safety is a component of the final course grade, and negligence can result in a student being dropped from the course.

## Biosafety level and hosts

The teaching lab operates at Biosafety Level 2 (BSL-2), as the research lab does. The bacterial hosts are the Risk Group 1 (RG-1) organisms *Mycobacterium smegmatis* mc²155 and *Microbacterium foliorum*. Culture plates and tubes containing biological agents are stored in secondary containment bins, and no biological agent, including contaminated material, leaves the lab.

## Lab rules

- **Electronic devices.** Each student research group has a dedicated laptop for the Phage Discovery Guide and the online electronic lab notebook, and the lab has dedicated digital cameras and ink markers for labeling and imaging tube contents, plaques and gels. Lab devices never leave the lab because of contamination, and personal electronic devices are not used inside it.
- **Personal protective equipment.** Disposable lab coats and gloves of all sizes and two sizes of safety glasses are provided. PPE contaminated with a biological agent or chemical goes into the autoclave bags. Clean PPE is stored in your lab space cabinet, and safety glasses go back to the lab manager after each session for sanitation. PPE is never worn out of the lab.
- **Glass culture tubes** go into the red biohazard sharps container, and nothing else goes into it.
- **Culture plates, tubes, syringes, filters, pipettes and pipette tips** that touched a biological agent go into the autoclave bags, and nothing else (such as clean wrappers or packaging) goes into them.
