Dustin Edwards leads the Department of Biological Sciences at Tarleton State University as Department Head, and teaches the two course-based research courses of the HHMI SEA-PHAGES program there, one per semester. In them, undergraduates isolate, name, sequence and analyze viruses that infect bacteria. This page lists those courses, the tutorials, and what joining the lab for undergraduate research involves. The earlier lecture-course pages (virology, genetics, vaccines, cell biology) have been retired and now point here.

## Courses

### Phage Discovery Program

The [Phage Discovery Program](/teaching/phage-discovery) is Tarleton's part of [SEA-PHAGES](https://seaphages.org/), a national collaboration in bacteriophage genomics run by the Science Education Alliance at the Howard Hughes Medical Institute (HHMI) with a team at the University of Pittsburgh. It is a two-semester set of discovery-based undergraduate research courses, both taught as Special Topics (BIOL 4090).

### Virus Isolation Course

The [Virus Isolation Course](/teaching/virus-isolation) is the first semester. Students collect soil or water samples, isolate and purify bacteriophages, image them by electron microscopy and extract their DNA for sequencing.

### Phage Bioinformatics course

The [Phage Bioinformatics course](/teaching/phage-bioinformatics) is the second semester. Students annotate the whole genomes of the phages sequenced from the first semester.

## Tutorials

The [Central Dogma Tutorials](/teaching/central-dogma) cover the molecular biology processes of encoding genetic information from DNA to mRNA to proteins.

## Undergraduate research opportunities

Authentic, mentored undergraduate research is a transformative, high-impact experience. Its benefits include connecting classroom theories to actual practice, learning valuable laboratory techniques, developing critical analysis skills, and training in scientific writing and presentation. Current projects are on the [research](/research) page.

Research in the lab carries more responsibilities than a course. Project proposals and protocols usually must first be approved by Tarleton's Institutional Biosafety Committee (IBC), Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) and/or Institutional Review Board (IRB), and all work follows The Texas A&M University System policies and regulations, Tarleton State University rules and procedures, and state and federal laws and regulations. Joining involves:

- **Training.** Regular safety training through the [CITI Program](https://about.citiprogram.org/) (valid for three years) and Texas A&M University System [TrainTraq](https://traintraq.tamus.edu/) (valid for one year), in the courses the project requires, such as Responsible Conduct of Research, biosafety, lab safety and bloodborne pathogens. Training records must be current and kept in the Laboratory Biosafety Manual.
- **Occupational health.** Enrollment in the Biosafety Occupational Health Program (BOHP).
- **Lab access.** The research lab normally operates at Biosafety Level 2 (BSL-2), with restricted, logged swipe-card entry and keys for specific equipment and chemicals. Swipe-card access must be approved by Dr. Dustin Edwards and by the Dean of the College of Science and Technology. After-hours access needs the same approvals and registration with the Tarleton State University Police Department.
