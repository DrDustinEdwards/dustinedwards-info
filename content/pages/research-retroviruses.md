This page covers Dustin Edwards's retrovirus research in two parts. The human work is on human T-cell leukemia virus type 1 (HTLV-1): how its Tax oncoprotein and its small accessory proteins help the virus transform cells, escape the immune response and persist in the host. The avian work is on two retroviruses of wild birds, reticuloendotheliosis virus (REV) and lymphoproliferative disease virus (LPDV), in wild turkeys and endangered prairie chickens. Each study below links to its paper page, and the full list is on the [publications page](/research/publications).

## Retroviruses in brief

Retroviruses copy their RNA genome into DNA and insert that DNA, the provirus, into the host cell's chromosomes. An infected host carries the provirus for life. That lifelong integration is what lets some retroviruses cause cancer, and it is also what makes them detectable by PCR on DNA from a small blood sample.

## Human T-cell leukemia virus type 1 (HTLV-1)

HTLV-1 is the cause of adult T-cell leukemia (ATL), an aggressive and highly chemoresistant malignancy. Besides its structural genes, the 3' end of the HTLV-1 genome carries overlapping open reading frames (ORFs) that encode regulatory and accessory proteins: Tax, the viral oncoprotein, and the orf-I and orf-II products p12, p8, p13 and p30. The accessory proteins are not needed for the virus to replicate in cell culture, but they matter for establishing and keeping infection in a living host.

### Tax and cell transformation

- Tax relieves repression of the proliferating cell nuclear antigen (PCNA) gene. A cellular complex binds a site within the Tax-responsive element of the PCNA promoter and acts as a repressor, and Tax expression blocks that complex and raises promoter activity ([Human T-Cell Leukemia Virus Type 1 Tax Relieves Repression of Proliferating Cell Nuclear Antigen Gene Expression](/research/publications/10-1128-jvi-00356-08/)).
- A textbook chapter describes HTLV-1 as a prototypic RNA tumor virus whose Tax oncoprotein targets regulators of the cell cycle and DNA repair ([RNA Tumour Viruses](/research/publications/10-1002-9780470025079-chap06-pub2/)).
- The geranylgeranyl transferase-I inhibitor GGTI-298 decreased the viability of HTLV-1-transformed cells, caused them to accumulate in G2/M, inhibited HTLV-1 LTR transcription and lowered Tax protein levels and NF-kappaB activation ([Inhibition of Geranylgeranyl Transferase-I Decreases Cell Viability of HTLV-1-Transformed Cells](/research/publications/10-3390-v3101815/)).

### The orf-I and orf-II proteins: p12, p8, p13 and p30

- A review summarizes what is known about the orf-I products p12 and p8 and the orf-II products p13 and p30, and their roles in transcription, apoptosis, T-cell activation, infectivity, transmission and immune responses ([Orf-I and Orf-II-Encoded Proteins in HTLV-1 Infection and Persistence](/research/publications/10-3390-v3060861/)).
- p30, but not p12 or p8, counteracts Toll-like receptor 3 (TLR3) and TLR4 signaling in human monocytes and dendritic cells, suppressing interferon-responsive genes ([HTLV-1 p30, but Not p12/p8, Counteracts TLR3 and TLR4 Signaling in Human Monocytes and Dendritic Cells](/research/publications/10-1128-jvi-01788-13/)).
- p8 and p12 share a conserved cysteine at position 39. Mutating it blocks their dimerization and palmitoylation, but p8 still reaches the cell surface, increases cell adhesion and supports virus transmission ([Palmitoylation and p8-Mediated Human T-Cell Leukemia Virus Type 1 Transmission](/research/publications/10-1128-jvi-03444-13/)). A conference abstract reported earlier work on the same question ([Role of dimerization and palmitoylation on the function of HTLV-1 p12 and p8](/research/publications/10-1186-1742-4690-8-s1-a124/)).
- p12 and p8 are codependent. Natural variation in orf-I changes how much of each protein the virus makes, balanced expression was associated with higher viral DNA loads in infected people, and in macaques only the virus that made both proteins established infection efficiently ([Co-dependence of HTLV-1 p12 and p8 Functions in Virus Persistence](/research/publications/10-1371-journal-ppat-1004454/)). The same finding was presented as a conference talk ([Both orf-I isoforms, p12 and p8, are required for efficient HTLV-1 infection and persistence](/research/publications/10-1186-1742-4690-11-s1-o13/)).

### Related viruses and tools

- In simian T-cell lymphotropic virus type 3 (STLV-3), almost identical to HTLV-3, three previously unknown viral mRNAs encoding putative accessory proteins were found and the location of each protein in the cell was characterized ([Discovery and Characterization of Auxiliary Proteins Encoded by Type 3 Simian T-Cell Lymphotropic Viruses](/research/publications/10-1128-jvi-02150-14/)).
- A public database collects and annotates published HTLV-1 sequences for molecular epidemiology and data mining ([A Public HTLV-1 Molecular Epidemiology Database for Sequence Management and Data Mining](/research/publications/10-1371-journal-pone-0042123/)).
- A conference poster examined HIV-1 and HTLV-1 regulatory proteins in coinfection ([Role of regulatory proteins in HIV-1/HTLV-1 coinfection](/research/publications/10-1186-1742-4690-11-s1-p122/)).

## Avian retroviruses: REV and LPDV

**Reticuloendotheliosis virus (REV)** is an immunosuppressive and sometimes oncogenic avian retrovirus that establishes lifelong infection in a wide range of bird species, including chickens, turkeys, ducks and prairie chickens. REV can also integrate into the genome of fowlpox virus, so a fowlpox infection can carry REV with it.

**Lymphoproliferative disease virus (LPDV)** is an avian retrovirus of turkeys. It has been reported in wild turkeys in the eastern United States and in states bordering Texas.

Both viruses can cause tumors, and the disease they cause looks similar. Telling them apart takes a molecular test, which is why the work screens for both.

### Why wild turkeys and prairie chickens?

The Attwater's prairie chicken (*Tympanuchus cupido attwateri*) is a highly endangered bird whose survival depends on captive breeding. An REV outbreak at a captive breeding facility in Texas caused a large share of adult prairie chicken deaths there. In wild birds, REV infection is usually uncommon and disease is rarely seen, so the outbreak raised a question: were wild birds living near the captive flock acting as a reservoir for the virus?

Wild turkeys were the natural group to test. They are galliform birds like prairie chickens, they range widely across Texas, and they include both the native Rio Grande wild turkey (*Meleagris gallopavo intermedia*) and Eastern wild turkeys (*Meleagris gallopavo silvestris*) imported for conservation. Imported birds could bring viruses, including LPDV, from regions where they are established.

### Surveillance for REV and LPDV

The surveillance work tests blood samples from wild turkeys for proviral DNA by PCR. REV is detected by amplifying the viral 3' long terminal repeat (LTR) and segments of the viral *pol* gene. The later study tested dried blood spots for both REV and LPDV. Positive products are sequenced so the viruses can be compared with strains from other regions.

The published surveys found:

- REV in native Rio Grande wild turkeys in Texas, and in an Eastern wild turkey that had been imported during conservation efforts ([Survey of Reticuloendotheliosis Virus in Wild Turkeys in Texas, USA](/research/publications/10-7589-2018-08-187/)).
- The first molecular evidence of LPDV in Texas, with continued detection of REV and a bird coinfected with both viruses. The Texas LPDV sequences were closely related to variants from other Southern and Central states ([Molecular Surveillance for Lymphoproliferative Disease Virus and Reticuloendotheliosis Virus in Rio Grande Wild Turkeys in Texas, USA](/research/publications/10-7589-jwd-d-22-00023/)).
- REV in Muscovy ducks, wild turkeys and chickens in northern Brazil, the first report of the virus in the Amazon biome, with strains closely related to variants in the United States ([Detection of Reticuloendotheliosis Virus in Muscovy Ducks, Wild Turkeys, and Chickens in Brazil](/research/publications/10-7589-2019-04-088/)).

Continued surveillance is needed to learn how these viruses affect wild turkey health and reproduction, and where outbreaks in captive flocks come from.

### Genetic studies

The genetic work sequences viral genomes to learn where the viruses come from and how they are related.

- A near-complete proviral REV genome from an Attwater's prairie chicken during the captive outbreak ([Near-Complete Proviral Genome Sequence of Reticuloendotheliosis Virus Isolated from an Attwater's Prairie Chicken](/research/publications/10-1128-mra-00173-22/)).
- A nearly intact REV provirus inserted within a field strain of fowlpox virus from a Rio Grande wild turkey ([Genome Sequence of Fowlpox Virus-Integrated Reticuloendotheliosis Virus from a Rio Grande Wild Turkey](/research/publications/10-1128-mra-00174-22/)).
