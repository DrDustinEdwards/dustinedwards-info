Enarratio (2026-09-29) is Latin and in no English dictionary read, so it was verified against Lewis and Short
(Perseus: https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.04.0059:entry=enarratio and :entry=enarro)
and Wiktionary (https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/enarratio, /wiki/enarro), and sense 1 against Quintilian,
Institutio Oratoria 1.4.2-3 and 1.9.1 (Latin: The Latin Library; English: Butler's Loeb on LacusCurtius).
Quintilian names grammar's two parts as correct speech and poetarum enarratio, and says correct reading comes
before enarratio, so the word is the explanation, not the reading aloud. Its American pronunciation is derived,
not quoted: the Latin stress (on -ra-, since the -ti- is short) with the vowels English speakers give restored
Latin. The clip at /audio/enarratio.mp3 was generated once with Workers AI (Deepgram Aura 2, voice "thalia") and
transcribed back to check it.
